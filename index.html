Calm Is Good. Calm and Quick? Let's Vamoos.

Nobody's asking you to panic, hustle or reply to emails at midnight. This is about something smaller and more useful: moving the moment something lands on your desk, because in travel, the window to win is short.

📌 Keep the calm. Lose the "I'll get to it later".
30sversion
  • Your calm is an asset. Keep it. Just point it at the things with a clock on them.
  • Quotes and consultations: within 24 hours. Payments and complaints: straight away.
  • You don't need the answer to reply. Acknowledge, set a time, then hit it.

02This Isn't About Rushing

Let's clear that up

Not letting things rattle you is a genuine strength. Clients don't want a frazzled consultant, and nobody does good work in a constant state of stress. So keep that.

A sense of urgency is something different. It's not stress, and it's not speed for the sake of looking busy. It's simply knowing which things have a clock on them, and acting before the clock runs out.

The best finishers are calm in front of goal. They still shoot quickly.

Take one touch too many and the defender closes the gap. Composure is what makes the shot good; speed is what makes it count. Keep the composure, add the quick release.

Too chill

"On it, back to you shortly"

03Why Travel Moves Fast

The clock isn't ours, it's the market's

In a lot of jobs, a day's delay doesn't change much. In sports travel, it can change everything, because most of what we sell is limited and moving.

What movesWhat that means for you
PricesFlight fares and hotel rates change constantly, especially close to big events. The price you quoted on Monday may not exist on Thursday.
AvailabilityTickets, hospitality packages and hotel rooms near the venue are limited. When they're gone, they're gone, no matter how good the quote looked.
The client's attentionClients often enquire with more than one company. The excitement is highest when they first reach out, and the first helpful reply usually sets the bar.
Holds and deadlinesSupplier holds, payment dates and document deadlines are fixed. Missing one can mean losing the booking, not just delaying it.
The fixture won't wait The final whistle, the chequered flag, the match point, they all happen on a set date. Our job is to get the client there, and every step before that date has its own little deadline.

05Not Every Message Has the Same Clock

Our response standard

Quote or consultation
24 hrs

The ceiling, not the target. Sooner is better on a new enquiry, while the client is excited and still shopping around.

Payment query
Now

Someone wondering whether their money landed, or what they owe, is anxious. Waiting a day is not okay here.

Complaint
Now

An upset client left waiting gets more upset. Reply straight away, even if the fix takes longer.

You don't need the answer to reply For payments and complaints, the first reply is about managing expectations: "I've seen this, I'm on it, and I'll update you by [time]." Then update them by that time, even if it's just to say you're still on it.

06What Vamoos Looks Like Day to Day

Small habits, not big speeches

  • Acknowledge fast, even if you can't answer yet. "Got it, I'm checking availability and will come back to you this afternoon" buys you time and keeps the client with you.
  • Call first, then move the stage. A call gets more done in five minutes than a thread of emails gets done in a day.
  • Do the two-minute things now. If a reply, a stage update or a note takes less than two minutes, it doesn't go on a list. It just gets done.
  • Never park without a date. If a quote is parked, there's a future task with a recontact date on it. "Later" is not a date.
  • Watch the clock that matters. Know which of your enquiries have a hold expiring, a price likely to move, or an event coming up fast, and do those first.
  • Close the loop. If you said you'd come back by a certain time, come back by then, even if it's just to say you're still on it.
  • Start the day with what's time-sensitive, not with what's easiest or most fun.

07Urgent or Not?

Have a think before you reveal the answer

Scenario 1. A new enquiry for a Grand Prix lands at 4:30pm. You're tired and planning to leave at 5.
Urgent, but small. You don't need to build the full quote tonight. A quick call or message to acknowledge it and ask your key questions takes a few minutes, keeps you well inside the 24-hour standard, and means you're first in their inbox tomorrow.
Scenario 2. A client has asked you to tweak the font on their itinerary PDF. You also have a supplier hold expiring tomorrow morning.
The hold wins. The font can wait an hour. The hold can't. Urgency is partly about choosing the right thing first.
Scenario 3. A client says they'll think about the quote and get back to you "sometime next week".
Not urgent today, but it needs a date. Park it with a future task and a specific recontact day. Calm is fine. Forgetting is not.
Scenario 4. A client emails to say they're still waiting on something you promised yesterday. You're busy with a new enquiry.
Urgent, right now. A broken promise hurts trust fast. Reply straight away, own it, and give them a time you'll actually hit.
Scenario 5. A client asks whether their deposit has come through. You need to check with accounts, who won't get back to you until tomorrow.
Reply now, not tomorrow. The 24 hours doesn't apply to payments. Let the client know straight away that you're checking with accounts and when they'll hear from you, so they're not left wondering where their money is.

Your one thing

Pick one habit from section 06 that you don't do yet. Do it every day for the next two weeks. That's it. Calm stays, later goes.
